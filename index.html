✨ What This Tool Does

Most people take a photo of their ID card with a phone and send it as-is. That photo often shows the full card number, the exact birth date, the address, and the signature. Once it is in someone else’s inbox, you lose control of it. id-watermark gives you two simple tools to fix that before you hit send:

Watermarking. You type a short sentence such as “For bank account opening only” or “For apartment rental, March 2025”. The tool lays that sentence across the photo in a repeated diagonal pattern so nobody can crop it away. If the picture is copied and reused somewhere else, the text travels with it and makes the reuse obvious.

Pixelating. You drag a box over the parts you want to hide. The tool turns that area into a mosaic of blocks so the original digits and words are unreadable. This is the same idea as blurring a face in a photo, but applied to your private numbers.

You can use one of these tools on its own, or both together. A typical result is an ID photo where the card number is a blocky grey rectangle and a clear watermark sentence runs across the whole image. That image is still accepted by most offices, because the name and photo are readable while the sensitive parts are protected.

🖱️ Using the App, Step by Step

Step 1 - Load your photo. Open the app and choose the picture of your ID card from your computer. You can also drag the file straight into the window. Nothing happens until you pick a file, so it is safe to open the app first and look around.

Step 2 - Write your watermark text. In the text box, type the sentence you want to appear on the image. Keep it short and specific. “For XYZ Company recruitment only” is much stronger than “sample”. A date helps too, because it shows when the copy was made.

Step 3 - Adjust how it looks. Make the text bigger or smaller, change how transparent it is, and pick how often it repeats. You want it easy to read but not so heavy that the office cannot see your name. A light grey or semi-transparent colour usually works best.

Step 4 - Hide the private parts. Switch to the pixelate tool and draw a rectangle over each area you want to hide - the card number, the address, the birth date, the signature. You can add as many boxes as you need, and you can remove one box without starting over.

Step 5 - Save the result. Press the save button and choose where the finished picture should go. Give it a new name so you keep the untouched original separate. Send the saved copy, never the original.

Step 6 - Check before you send. Open the saved picture once and zoom in. Make sure the pixel blocks fully cover the numbers and that the watermark text is readable. If something is still visible, go back and add another block.

Questions People Ask

Will the office accept a watermarked copy? In most cases yes. A watermark that says what the copy is for is usually seen as a sign that you are careful with your data. If a company insists on an unmarked copy, ask them why and how they will store it.

Can someone remove the watermark? A determined person with advanced tools can do a lot, but the point is different. The watermark makes casual reuse obvious and shows that the copy was made for one specific purpose. That is enough protection for everyday situations.

Do I need to create an account? No account, no email, no password.

Does it work on phone photos? Yes. Photos taken with a phone are just image files, and the tool treats them the same as any other picture.

🛠️ If Something Goes Wrong

The download does not start. Check that you are on the download page and that your browser is not blocking the file. Look for a small icon near the address bar that says something was blocked, and allow it.

The page looks blank. Reload the page once. If it stays blank, try a different browser - Edge and Chrome are the safest choices on Windows.

My photo will not open. Make sure it is a normal image file such as JPG or PNG. If you took it with a phone, it may be HEIC format; save or export it as JPG first, then try again.

The saved picture looks too blurry. Pixel blocks are meant to be blurry, but if the rest of the picture looks soft, start again from the original file rather than from an already-saved copy. Saving a copy of a copy reduces quality each time.

The watermark text is hard to read. Increase the text size or choose a darker colour. Check that transparency is not set too low. Save, reopen, and zoom in to confirm.

💡 Good Habits for Sharing ID Copies

The tool is only one part of staying safe. A few simple habits make a big difference:

  • Write a specific purpose every time. “For car rental on 12 April” is better than “for official use”.
  • Always pixelate the card number, the address, and the signature unless the receiver genuinely needs them.
  • Keep the original photo on your own device and send only the marked copy.
  • Delete old copies from your sent mail and your downloads folder once the request is finished.
  • If a company asks for an unmarked copy, ask them to explain how they protect it.
